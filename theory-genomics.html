LESSON 01

Genomics Overview

Genomics studies the complete genetic material of an organism and how its components interact. Unlike genetics, which often focuses on individual genes, genomics examines genome-wide patterns and variation.

DNA
The molecule that stores hereditary information
Gene
A functional genomic region producing RNA or protein
Genome
The complete coding and non-coding DNA sequence
Key point: A reference genome is a coordinate system for analysis, not a sequence representing every individual.

LESSON 03

Genomic Variant Analysis

The analysis converts sequencing signals into a reliable set of variants while controlling technical artifacts.

Quality control→Alignment→Post-processing→Variant calling→Annotation
  • SNV: a single-nucleotide change
  • Indel: a small insertion or deletion
  • CNV: a gain or loss in copy number
  • SV: a larger rearrangement such as inversion or translocation
Key point: Variant calling produces candidates. Read depth, allele balance, mapping quality, and technical context determine confidence.
LESSON 04

Variant Interpretation

Interpretation combines population frequency, predicted functional consequence, biological evidence, and clinical context.

Germline variant

Present in inherited DNA and potentially shared across tissues and family members.

Somatic variant

Acquired in a subset of cells and commonly evaluated using matched tumor and normal samples.

  1. Confirm technical reliability.
  2. Check population frequency.
  3. Assess gene and protein consequence.
  4. Review disease and clinical evidence.
  5. Classify with appropriate guidelines.
Key point: A statistically detected variant is not automatically pathogenic, causal, or clinically actionable.
